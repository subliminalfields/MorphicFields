Karma is a fundamental concept in many Eastern philosophies, particularly within Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism. Derived from the Sanskrit word meaning "action" or "deed," karma refers to the principle of cause and effect, where every action has a corresponding reaction.

Basic Principle: Every action (physical, verbal, or mental) generates an energy that returns to us in kind. This cycle of actions and their results is what shapes our future experiences.

Consequences: Good actions create positive effects, leading to happiness and success, while negative actions lead to suffering and misfortune.

There are three different types of karma: prarabdha, sanchita, and kriyamana or agami.Prarabdha karma is experienced through the present body and is only a part of sanchita karma, which is the sum of one's past karmas, whereas agami karma is the result of current decisions and actions.

Sanchita Karma: The accumulated karma from all past lives. It is like a reservoir of all our actions.

Prarabdha Karma: The portion of sanchita karma that is being experienced in the present life. It’s what is destined to play out in the current lifetime.

Kriyamana Karma: The karma that we are creating in the present moment with our current actions. This will influence our future.

In simple words, this field would help you to burn all these types of karma. With an elegant and smooth technique (and some deities help) the seeds of karma will be ashes in the wind.
